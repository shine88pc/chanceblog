On October 27, 2026, Environment Commissioner Jessika Roswall launched the Friends of Credits for Nature and Biodiversity on behalf of the EU. The launch took place at an event at COP17, the 17th Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity, in Yerevan, Armenia. The announcement says the EU, France and Peru co-hosted that event, but in two other places it names only France and Peru as co-hosts, once for the side event and once for the Commission's high-level event on nature credits. The Commission's Directorate-General for Environment published [its account of the launch](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-launches-friends-credits-nature-and-biodiversity-2026-10-28_en) the next day, October 28.

### What the group is for

The Commission calls the group an informal, inclusive initiative led by governments. Its stated aims are to build international support for nature credits and to strengthen cooperation on biodiversity finance, giving governments a platform to work together on credible investment that protects nature.

The Commission describes nature credits as innovative financial instruments that attract investment in protecting, restoring and maintaining nature and that reward the people who do that work. It adds that they make nature-positive action more visible, credible and investable, and that they can help bring in private finance. The announcement also says governments remain central to biodiversity finance but their budgets are under pressure, and that investment by businesses and innovative financing tools, nature credits among them, can help close the global funding gap for biodiversity.

### Who is named

Besides France and Peru, the co-hosts of the side event, [the announcement](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-launches-friends-credits-nature-and-biodiversity-2026-10-28_en) welcomes Finland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Spain, Australia, Botswana, Canada, Chile, Indonesia, Japan and New Zealand, which makes 14 countries in all. It says other countries attended and expressed interest in joining but does not name them. It also says the initiative complements the work of international organisations such as the Biodiversity Credit Alliance, the International Advisory Panel on Biodiversity Credits, International Environmental Guardianship, the OECD and the IUCN, and that it is open to relevant international organisations.

### Background and next steps

The Commission puts its support to partners worldwide for biodiversity at an average of €1 billion a year. A roadmap towards nature credits, adopted in July 2025, sets out research, pilots, stakeholder consultation and practical testing to develop reliable credits.

Government members still have to formally endorse a joint statement, and the announcement gives no date for that. The EU also plans a side event on November 18 at COP31, the UN climate conference, to reflect the links between nature and climate. Roswall said the strong interest governments showed across regions, along with their experience and priorities, will shape future work.

### What the announcement does not say

The announcement does not compare nature credits with carbon credits, and it does not lay out standards for measuring them, rules for verifying them or safeguards against misuse. It speaks of credible and reliable credits but gives no detail beyond the roadmap's pilots and testing.
