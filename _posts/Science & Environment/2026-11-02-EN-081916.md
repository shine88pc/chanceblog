On October 29, 2026, NASA put out a Request for Information (RFI) asking industry what it can offer for in-space servicing that would support the Hubble Space Telescope. NASA's [announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasa-explores-potential-commercial-servicing-for-hubble-telescope/), published October 30 and updated November 3, says the RFI is posted online on SAM.gov, the federal contracting site, and sets a deadline of 3 p.m. EST on Monday, November 30 for responses.

This article is based on that announcement alone, read on November 9, 2026. An RFI asks for information, and the announcement does not report a contract award, a selected provider or an approved mission.

### What NASA Is Asking About

NASA wants to hear from commercial companies about three possibilities:

- a mission to raise Hubble to a higher altitude and prolong its science life;
- servicing intended to improve Hubble's capabilities; and
- a safe, controlled re-entry into the atmosphere, or orbital disposal, once Hubble's mission is over.

The announcement lists the three side by side. It does not rank them or say whether more than one would be pursued.

### Why the Calendar Matters

Hubble, an international project of NASA and the European Space Agency (ESA), has operated for more than three decades. Like any spacecraft in low Earth orbit, it is gradually losing altitude to atmospheric drag, and it carries no propulsion system that could keep its orbit up. NASA cites an analysis pointing to re-entry in 2033 or 2034, which is why it expects any mission would have to happen by 2032. The announcement does not say who carried out the analysis. The 2032 date is NASA's expected deadline for a mission, not a scheduled launch.

### What NASA Officials Said

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said Hubble has more important science to deliver. If innovative, cost-effective ways exist to keep a spacecraft that is already in orbit working longer, he said, NASA owes it to taxpayers and the scientific community to examine them. Nicky Fox, associate administrator of NASA's Science Mission Directorate, pointed to recent progress by commercial companies in capturing and servicing satellites in orbit. She said NASA wants to look at the full range of options for lifting Hubble's orbit in the time left before its expected re-entry, while the next flagship mission is developed. The announcement gives no cost figure.

### What the Announcement Does Not Say

The announcement names no company that would carry out a servicing mission, and it describes no selection criteria, contract schedule or funding level for any resulting mission. Apart from the November 30 response deadline and the 2032 date above, it gives no mission or contract timeline. The RFI itself was not opened for this article and may contain requirements the announcement does not summarize, so its technical content should be checked on SAM.gov. After the November 30 deadline, that listing and NASA's announcement page are the places to look for any further notice.
