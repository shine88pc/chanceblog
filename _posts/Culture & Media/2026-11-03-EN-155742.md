Even as social media platforms remain dominant channels for daily information, recent data highlights a notable shift in public confidence. Over the past year, Americans' trust in information provided by news organizations—both national and local—has risen across political parties and age groups, while trust in information from social media remains virtually unchanged.

### What the Data Establishes

According to findings published by the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/11/02/americans-trust-in-information-from-news-organizations-has-risen-in-the-past-year/) on November 2, 2026, public trust in information coming from national and local news outlets grew over the past year. This increase was observed broad-scale across party affiliations and different age demographics rather than being limited to a single partisan or generational segment.

By contrast, public trust in information obtained from social media platforms showed virtually no movement over the same period. The contrast underscores a clear boundary between where people spend time discovering content and where they place baseline trust for accurate news.

### Contextualizing Media Trust and Civic Life

Generally, social media platforms offer speed and algorithmically customized content feeds, but they also expose users to unverified claims and unreliable sources. Established news organizations, on the other hand, rely on structured reporting, source verification, and editorial oversight.

The fact that the rise in trust spans both national and local reporting across political lines suggests that news consumers across demographics are increasingly placing value on structured news gathering. From a media industry perspective, these findings emphasize that traditional editorial standards retain strong civic value even in a highly digitized attention economy.

### Broader Implications and Scope

These findings represent nonpartisan opinion research by the Pew Research Center and do not constitute an endorsement of specific policy interventions or media business models. Furthermore, an increase in trust does not automatically resolve underlying economic pressures on local journalism or reverse shifts in audience media habits.

However, for content creators, publishers, and audiences, the survey highlights a crucial dynamic: while platforms may control content distribution, verified reporting and editorial accountability remain central to earning long-term public confidence.
