Among U.S. political ads on Facebook and Instagram that mention data centers, 57% in October (through October 24) opposed data centers and 3% supported them. Those figures come from [an analysis Pew Research Center published on October 29](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/10/29/on-facebook-and-instagram-political-ads-are-increasingly-critical-of-data-centers/). The same analysis shows a very different January, when 42% of such ads were supportive and 31% opposed. The change over those ten months is the core of Pew's finding.

These are shares of ads, not shares of Americans: they describe what the ads argued, not what voters think. Unless marked as this site's own calculation, the figures below are those shown on Pew's page as of November 4, 2026.

### What Was Counted

- **Scope:** 20,751 ads published between January 1 and October 24, 2026, on Facebook and Instagram in the United States.
- **Method:** An AI language model assigned each ad a stance: opposed, supportive or neither. Checked against 300 ads coded by trained people, the model averaged an F1 score of 0.88 and 95% accuracy. (F1 weighs missed and wrongly flagged cases together; 1.0 is perfect.) Those scores combine stance with topic and candidate information, and a stance-only score is not given. Some misclassified ads should be expected.
- **The remainder:** Ads that were neither opposed nor supportive do not appear in Pew's table. Subtracting the two shares from 100% leaves about 40% of October's ads. That figure is this site's own subtraction, and rounding can move it by about a percentage point. Pew described this remainder for January as ads that took no clear position.

### How the Balance Moved

In January, supportive ads (42%) outnumbered opposed ads (31%). By February the order had flipped, with 52% opposed and 14% supportive. The opposed share then did not climb in a straight line: it fell to 42% in March, returned to 57% in April and May, peaked at 64% in June, stayed between 60% and 62% from July through September, and was back at 57% in October. The supportive share stayed between 3% and 8% from May on.

Pew's chart and downloadable data include no monthly ad counts, so it is not possible to tell how firm each month's percentage is.

### Reading the 3% Correctly

A 3% share does not mean supportive ads vanished. When opposed ads grow, the supportive share falls even if the number of supportive ads stays the same, and without monthly counts there is no way to tell which of the two happened.

### Where the Numbers Stop

- Ads after October 24 are not included.
- Only ads on Facebook and Instagram that Meta's ad library lists as social issue, electoral or political ads are covered. Other platforms and media are not.
- The figures count ads, not viewers. The numbers Pew reports do not include how many people saw the ads.
- Why advertisers chose their messages, and whether the ads influenced voters, is not addressed in the source.
- This is a U.S. case and does not carry over automatically to data center debates in other countries.
