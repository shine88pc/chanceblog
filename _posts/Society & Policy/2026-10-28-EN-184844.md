A [news item](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/reinforcing-frontex-stronger-protection-eus-external-borders-2026-10-27_en) dated October 27, 2026 says the European Commission has proposed strengthening Frontex, the EU agency that assists EU and Schengen countries in handling the Union's external borders. The Commission presents the plan as a way of giving Frontex suitable tools and resources so that it can act faster and with better results. The item names three main priorities and then describes a further proposal, on digitalising return procedures.

The item was published by the Commission's Directorate-General for Communication, so it is the Commission's own account of its proposals. This article rests on that single item. It reports what the item says was proposed and what happens next, and it does not judge the proposals. The press release the item links to, the legal texts and any reactions from governments or other institutions were not checked.

### The Three Priorities the Item Names

The Frontex proposal is organised under three headings:

- **Shared resources and operational capacity.** Frontex's standing corps would be built up step by step to 30,000 officers who could be deployed to help EU countries whenever they need it, and Frontex would find it easier to obtain cutting-edge equipment and technology.
- **A bigger role in returns.** Frontex would steer return operations across the EU in a more active and strategic way. A dedicated Return Coordination Centre is the proposed means of making Frontex's support to EU countries on returns as effective and economical as possible, and Frontex would also be able to help third countries with returns.
- **Crisis response.** An Online Monitoring and Response Centre would be set up to catch dangerous trends early and improve the way information is exchanged. Fast intervention in an emergency or crisis would come from a Quick Reaction Force that could be deployed.

Words such as faster, effective and cost-efficient describe what the Commission intends. The item offers no figures or evaluation showing that the changes would deliver those results.

### Digitalising Return Procedures

The item adds that the Commission has also proposed digitalising the procedures for return, readmission and reintegration. The aims it lists are to make the management of returns across the EU simpler, quicker and more modern, to reduce the administrative burden, to improve how data are gathered and shared, and to give Frontex a larger role in building a new digital architecture, at EU level, for EU countries. How the digital systems would work is not described.

### The Next Step, and What the Item Does Not Cover

The next move belongs to the European Parliament and the Council, which must agree on the proposals; the Commission says it will help the co-legislators move the negotiations forward. The item links the proposals to earlier pledges, those in the 2024–2029 political guidelines that President von der Leyen repeated in her 2026 State of the Union address.

Several details do not appear in the item: how many officers the standing corps has today and when it would reach 30,000, what the changes would cost and how they would be funded, the text of the proposals, and how large the Quick Reaction Force would be. It also reports no reaction from member states, the Parliament or other bodies. Readers who need those details can start with the press release linked from the item, which this article did not open.
