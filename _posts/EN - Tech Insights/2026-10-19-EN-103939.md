An “AI Native Factory” is a proposal to connect factory data and AI recommendations to operating decisions, rather than leave each model as a separate tool. In its [AW2026 announcement](https://onepredict.ai/news/?bmode=view&idx=171473659), OnePredict said it planned to present the concept at the March 2026 exhibition in Seoul. That announcement documents the company’s plan and product descriptions; it is not, by itself, evidence of a successful deployment or measured factory results.

## The system OnePredict describes

The company outlines three connected parts: cyclone prepares manufacturing data for AI use; its Manufacturing Foundation Model (MxFM) is intended to adapt to tasks such as maintenance, quality, and energy; and pdx is described as connecting operational systems across production lines, processes, and factories. The proposed shift is from equipment-level diagnostics toward a broader operating platform.

In June 2026, Edaily reported that OnePredict was working on an “AI Native Factory OS” to connect and coordinate manufacturing AI agents. Its account of pdx’s role is based on the company’s description, not an independent performance evaluation. A more detailed product roadmap is evidence of what the vendor is building toward; it does not show that a factory has achieved better output, quality, or energy use.

## A practical way to assess a pilot

“AI-centered operations” is a direction, not a measurable result. A buyer should start with one defined production problem. For predictive maintenance, track missed failures and false alarms alongside downtime. For quality inspection, specify product types, defect classes, and the cost of incorrect decisions. For energy optimization, compare energy per unit of output so that a production-volume change is not mistaken for an efficiency gain.

Then ask how equipment data is collected, which existing control or production systems must be integrated, who maintains the connections, and who approves or can override an AI recommendation. The operating plan should also explain what happens when the model is unavailable or wrong. A useful evaluation publishes the site and equipment covered, trial period, baseline, measurement method, integration costs, and ongoing support needs.

The public announcements describe a connected architecture and a company roadmap. They do not yet establish how well it performs across real factories, what it costs to operate, or whether the same approach generalizes across industries. Those questions require site-level evidence and follow-up reporting.
