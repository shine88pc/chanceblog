Japan’s Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft launched aboard H3 Flight 10 from Tanegashima on October 20, 2026, at 4:41 a.m. Japan time. JAXA’s mission website describes the flight and its planned five-year journey. The spacecraft is now in flight; its sample-return objectives remain future milestones.

MMX is designed to study Phobos and Deimos and return a sample from Phobos to Earth. JAXA says the spacecraft will collect at least 10 grams during two planned soft-landing operations. The mission plan targets a return to Earth in 2031. The sample has not yet been collected or returned.

## Why collect a sample from Phobos?

Phobos is one of Mars’s two moons. Its origin remains an open scientific question: it may be a captured asteroid or material formed after a large impact on Mars. MMX will study the moons and analyze returned material to provide evidence about the formation and evolution of the Martian system. The mission’s goals should not be confused with results that have yet to be obtained.

Sample return also tests technologies needed for planetary exploration: operating around a small moon, selecting a sampling location from observations, performing surface operations, and bringing material back to Earth. JAXA describes MMX as a multi-module spacecraft with propulsion, exploration, and return functions. European partners contribute instruments and the IDEFIX rover.

## What the sample can—and cannot—tell us

A sample can be examined with laboratory instruments that cannot be sent to Phobos, but its scientific value will depend on where and how it is collected and on contamination control. It will not, by itself, settle every question about the moons or establish that life existed there. JAXA frames the investigation around the origin and evolution of the Martian moons and the history of materials in the Mars system.

The next milestones are the spacecraft’s cruise and arrival, observations of Phobos and Deimos, the planned sampling operations, and the eventual return capsule. Schedules can change, so planned outcomes should be reported as plans until the mission confirms them.
