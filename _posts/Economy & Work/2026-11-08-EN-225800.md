Progress Residential, the largest property manager of single-family rental homes in the United States, along with its owner, private equity firm Pretium Partners, has agreed to pay $49.75 million to resolve charges concerning deceptive rental pricing practices and unlawful conduct. According to the [Federal Trade Commission press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/progress-residential-owner-pretium-partners-pay-4975-million-settle-ftc-states-charges-over), this represents the largest enforcement matter brought against a landlord in the Commission's history.

The action was filed jointly by the FTC and the state attorneys general of Arizona, Colorado, Indiana, and Utah. The complaint targets hidden fee structures and unfair operational practices that impacted renters across multiple states.

### Core Allegations in the Complaint

The enforcement action alleges that since at least 2021, Progress Residential and Pretium Partners misled prospective and current tenants through inaccurate pricing advertisements and undisclosed fees. Key allegations outlined by regulators include:

- **False Advertising and Hidden Fees:** Advertising lower base rents while concealing additional monthly fees that consumers often discovered only after signing a lease or moving in.
- **Failure to Inspect and Improper Deposit Deductions:** Neglecting to conduct pre-move-in property inspections and improperly deducting funds from security deposits when tenants moved out.
- **Restrictions on Reviews and Customer Service Deficits:** Restricting tenants from posting negative reviews and failing to provide adequate customer support for housing maintenance and inquiries.

Regulators stated that these undisclosed fees cumulative cost renters tens of millions of dollars over several years.

### Implications for Renters and Market Competition

For households evaluating housing choices, clear pricing is essential for comparison and financial planning. Undisclosed mandatory fees mask the true cost of tenancy, making it difficult for renters to accurately evaluate competing options in the market.

Christopher Mufarrige, Director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, stated that consumers searching for housing require straightforward information regarding rental costs. Straightforward pricing practices enable renters to make informed choices while encouraging housing providers to compete directly on price, home quality, and customer service.

As institutional ownership of single-family rentals has grown, regulatory scrutiny over fee disclosure and property management practices underscores the policy focus on transparency in consumer-facing housing transactions.
