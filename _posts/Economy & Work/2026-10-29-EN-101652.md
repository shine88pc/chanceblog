On October 28, 2026, the European Commission held its [first High-Level Dialogue with the European refining industry](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-launches-high-level-dialogue-european-oil-refining-sector-2026-10-28_en). The announcement, published by the Commission's Directorates-General for Energy and for Defence Industry and Space, presents the meeting as the start of a broader process of engagement with the sector. Dan Jørgensen, Commissioner for Energy and Housing, and Andrius Kubilius, Commissioner for Defence and Space, chaired it. Taking part were representatives of the oil refining sector, European refiners, emerging fuel producers, engineering companies, technology providers, industry associations and NATO.

### Why the Commission says refining matters

According to the announcement, EU refineries supply about 70% of the jet fuel and about 85% of the diesel consumed in Europe. The Commission treats oil refining as a strategic asset for energy security, industrial competitiveness and defence readiness, and it names ongoing disruptions caused by the conflict in the Middle East and a fast-changing geopolitical environment as the backdrop.

The topics it lists include the short-term response to the disruptions caused by the conflict in the Middle East, the medium-term competitiveness and transformation of European refineries, getting the most out of existing refining capacity, the resilience of fuel supply chains, and enough production capacity to meet rising demand, including in crises and for defence. It names high energy costs, global competition and the investment needed for sustainable biofuels and e-fuels as pressures on the sector.

### What the two commissioners said

Jørgensen called the dialogue a first step. He said the Commission will work to raise output from refineries in Europe because fuel prices need to come down, and that it will work with the industry on a joint roadmap for the transformation of refining. Kubilius stressed that fuel supply is a large part of military logistics.

### What is and is not settled

The announcement describes the meeting as the start of a broader engagement process and says the results should help to identify shared priorities and possible actions that support the sector's move to clean energy and its long-term viability in Europe. Jørgensen's remarks in the announcement add a joint roadmap that the Commission will work on with the industry. It gives no date for another meeting, no timetable for the roadmap and no frequency for further talks, and it does not describe the dialogue as regular or standing. It also announces no regulatory change, subsidy, funding or other concrete measure, and it does not say how output from European refineries would be raised.
