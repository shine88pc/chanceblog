KIMM’s Southeast Regional Division used its “OPEN LAB” event to put researchers, companies, and regional industry in the same room. KIMM says the third event took place on October 14, 2026, at the Busan Global Tech Biz Center, with 67 people from 28 industry, academic, and research organizations. The event details are in [KIMM’s announcement](https://www.kimm.re.kr/sub0504/view/id/21491), also listed by the [National Research Council of Science & Technology (NST)](https://www.nst.re.kr/www/selectBbsNttView.do?bbsNo=13&key=62&nttNo=52487). Attendance and presentations show that a discussion happened; they do not establish that a factory adopted a system or improved production.

## What participants were invited to examine

The program covered industrial applications of digital twins, digital transformation (DX), and AI transformation (AX). Named presentations included LLM-agent orchestration for multi-domain computer-aided engineering (CAE), KIMM Cyber Lab, knowledge structuring for AI-agent collaboration, and manufacturing AI applications. KIMM also introduced support connected with laser processing, testing and certification for future-mobility components, and nuclear-power equipment. The institute says it introduced international technology-cooperation support and surveyed participating companies about needs such as overseas-market discovery, research partnerships, and joint projects. A lab tour followed for interested participants.

Those details make the event more concrete than a general showcase: it linked technical talks with research facilities and possible business-support routes. KIMM’s wider [corporate-support programs](https://www.kimm.re.kr/etsc/biz-support-overview) include technical assistance, commercialization, equipment access, and staffing support. These are available pathways described by the institute, not evidence that every attendee received support or that a particular AI system was deployed.

## How a company can evaluate the next step

For a manufacturer, a useful pilot begins with a defined production problem and a baseline. A predictive-maintenance trial should report missed failures and false alarms as well as downtime; visual quality inspection should disclose the defect types and inspection conditions; process optimization should track energy use and yield alongside output. The measures need a time period and a comparison method so that normal variation is not mistaken for an AI effect.

Companies also need to ask how often data arrives, which equipment and software must be connected, who handles integration and ongoing maintenance, and whether workers can review or override a recommendation. The event announcement does not provide pilot results, operating costs, or before-and-after measurements. Those would be the evidence needed to judge business value. An open lab can help start the conversation, but follow-up trials and transparent evaluation must establish whether a tool works on a real production line.
