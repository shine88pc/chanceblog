# The Tragic Cost of Incomplete Reform: Why Firefighters Remain Under-Protected

The recent surge in line-of-duty deaths among South Korean firefighters has reignited a fierce national debate over public safety infrastructure. With four tragic deaths recorded already this year—and 28 fallen first responders over the last seven years—public grief has transformed into urgent calls for systemic reform.

At the center of this controversy lies a glaring institutional mismatch: **while South Korean firefighters were officially reclassified as national public servants in 2020, their operational budgets remain overwhelmingly reliant on local regional governments.**

## The 1-to-9 Asymmetry: National in Title, Municipal in Pocket

In April 2020, South Korea transitioned tens of thousands of municipal firefighters to national civil servant status. The landmark reform was celebrated as a historic step toward closing regional gaps in equipment, manpower, and safety standards.

However, the financial mechanism behind the reform was largely left unresolved:

* **Budget Dependency:** Nearly 90% of firefighting budgets still come from local municipalities, while the national government covers only around 10%.
* **Disparity by Region:** Wealthier provinces and metropolitan cities can afford state-of-the-art protective gear and adequate personnel rotations, whereas financially strained regions struggle to replace aging respirators and specialized gear.
* **Legislative Impasse:** The National Assembly Research Service has repeatedly highlighted that without direct central budget appropriations, the "nationalization" of firefighters remains a half-measure.

Recent political friction over regional budget burdens and controversial rhetoric comparing firefighting subsidies to fiscal "straws" have highlighted the unresolved tension between the central government and local authorities.

## Modern Fire Hazards vs. Outdated Logistical Support

Modern urban environments present unprecedented dangers. From sprawling high-rise residential towers and battery warehouses to advanced industrial materials that emit extreme toxic gases during combustion, tactical firefighting requires cutting-edge protective gear and seamless communication equipment.

When local councils face budget deficits, capital expenditures for specialized gear and safety reserves are often the first to face delays. This leaves frontline responders exposed to heightened operational risks, extreme exhaustion from understaffed rotation shifts, and fatal operational accidents.

## What Needs to Change

To honor the sacrifices of fallen first responders and ensure the safety of those still serving, experts and frontline unions point to several essential reforms:

1. **Unified National Financing:** A dedicated federal allocation mechanism must replace the precarious reliance on the Special Safety Grant Tax, ensuring a guaranteed central budget floor for all regional commands.
2. **Centralized Procurement:** Procurement of personal protective equipment (PPE) should be standardized and directly managed by the National Fire Agency to eliminate quality disparities between prefectures.
3. **Institutional Accountability:** The central government must take full fiscal and operational responsibility for firefighter safety protocols, training facilities, and long-term health monitoring.

Reclassifying personnel status on paper is meaningless if the boots on the ground still depend on local municipal surpluses. Protecting those who protect the nation requires genuine, unified national investment.
