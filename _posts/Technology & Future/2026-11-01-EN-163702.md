According to an announcement published by the European Commission's Digital Strategy initiative, the Local Digital Twins CiTiVERSE EDIC (LDT CiTiVERSE EDIC) will participate in the [Smart City Expo World Congress 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/events/local-digital-twins-citiverse-edic-joins-smart-city-expo-world-congress-2026). The event will take place from November 3 to November 5, 2026, at Fira de Barcelona (Hall 3, Stand D71). The organization plans to showcase human-centric artificial intelligence (AI) solutions designed for smart cities.

### Mapping Physical Cities to Digital Environments

In general terms, a local digital twin is a virtual 3D representation of a city's physical assets, transportation networks, energy infrastructure, and environmental conditions. By coupling real-world sensor streams and statistical data with virtual models, urban planners can simulate policy adjustments, infrastructure changes, and disaster preparedness scenarios prior to physical implementation.

The centerpiece of LDT CiTiVERSE EDIC's presentation is human-centric AI. In smart city design, human-centric systems prioritize accessibility, privacy, and community wellbeing alongside algorithmic efficiency, ensuring that automated decision-support tools remain aligned with public needs.

### Trade-offs, Costs, and Impact on Daily Life

Deploying AI-assisted local digital twin frameworks involves tangible changes for municipal administrators and residents:

- **Civic Experience**: Residents benefit from improved public transit planning, reduced traffic bottlenecks during construction, and optimized energy distribution during peak periods.
- **Fiscal Efficiency**: Municipalities can test major urban policies virtually, reducing trial-and-error costs before committing public funds to physical construction.
- **Cost Allocation and Data Protection**: Establishing and maintaining high-density sensor networks, data standardization, and cybersecurity protections requires ongoing public investment and strict compliance with privacy standards.

### Analytical Limitations in Urban Governance

While digital twin simulations offer detailed quantitative forecasts, virtual models cannot capture every social variable or community preference. Data outputs serve as decision-support mechanisms for city officials rather than substitutes for public consultation and civic oversight.

The participation of LDT CiTiVERSE EDIC at the Smart City Expo World Congress 2026 highlights how European digital infrastructure frameworks are steering urban AI applications toward human-centered policy design.
