Statistics Canada's [release of November 6, 2026](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/daily-quotidien/261106/dq261106b-eng.htm) reports that mortality among adults who were recently incarcerated in five provinces more than doubled between 2016 and 2023, from 94 deaths per 10,000 people to 230 per 10,000. The provinces are Nova Scotia, Ontario, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia, and the figures cover people who passed through their provincial correctional services. The release's first sentence makes the broader point that people who have been incarcerated in Canada face a higher risk of premature death than the general population.

### The count for people aged 20 to 49

The release's headline figure is 1,790 deaths in 2023 among people aged 20 to 49 who had been incarcerated in 2022 or 2023. That is more than twice the 2016 count. In 2023, men in their 20s and 30s with a history of incarceration died at about 18 times the rate of men of the same age who had not been incarcerated.

### Where and how the deaths happened

Between 2016 and 2023, 96% of the deaths occurred in the community after release and 4% while people were still in custody. Counting only deaths within up to two years of release, the average gap between release and death was 156 days, and the median was 198 days. Accidental drug toxicity was the most common underlying cause, and its share of all deaths rose from 40% in 2016 to 61% in 2021. Homicide was also far more common among men aged 20 to 49 with a history of incarceration than among men of that age who had not been incarcerated.

### What the numbers do not cover

These are not national figures. They describe adults who went through provincial correctional services in five provinces, and the release does not address federal institutions, the other provinces or the territories.

Death investigations can take years, so some deaths now recorded with an unknown or unspecified cause may later be attributed to accidental drug toxicity or another cause. For that reason Statistics Canada says the current drug toxicity share could turn out higher than the recorded figure.

The release documents patterns and causes of death but does not explain why mortality rose or why deaths cluster after release, and this article does not speculate. The release points to a Juristat analysis for more on methods; we did not open it.
