If a date printed on food has passed, a smell test or an online chart saying “a few more days” cannot establish that the product is safe. On Korean labels, **유통기한 (yutong-gihan)** refers to the period in which a product may be sold, while **소비기한 (sobi-gihan)** is the date through which it can be safely consumed when the stated storage instructions are followed. They answer different questions.

## What the two dates tell you

South Korea moved from the sale-oriented 유통기한 to the consumer-oriented 소비기한 on 1 January 2023, when an amendment to the [Act on Labeling and Advertising of Food, etc.](https://www.law.go.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%EB%93%B1%EC%9D%98%ED%91%9C%EC%8B%9C%E3%86%8D%EA%B4%91%EA%B3%A0%EC%97%90%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0) (식품 등의 표시·광고에 관한 법률, title translated informally; Act No. 18445, promulgated 17 August 2021) took effect. It defined 소비기한 and replaced 유통기한 with 소비기한 in the date item printed on food, which the law now words as manufacturing date, 소비기한, or quality-retention date (Article 4(1), item 1, sub-item 라). The Act defines 소비기한 as the date through which food is safe to eat when the storage method stated on it is observed (Article 2, item 12). The amendment’s [statement of reasons](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsRvsRsnListP.do?lsId=013094&lsRvsGubun=all&chrClsCd=010102) describes 유통기한 as the period, counted from manufacture, in which sale to consumers is permitted.

There were transitional rules. The new date item applies to food manufactured, processed or loaded for import on or after that day ([Addenda, Article 2(1) (Act No. 18445 entry)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsRvsDocListP.do?lsId=013094&chrClsCd=010202&lsRvsGubun=all)), and the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety’s [labeling standards notice](https://www.law.go.kr/%ED%96%89%EC%A0%95%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99/%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%EB%93%B1%EC%9D%98%ED%91%9C%EC%8B%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80) (식품등의 표시기준) lets food made or shipped earlier be sold until its old 유통기한 (Addenda to Notice No. 2022-25, Article 3). In this guide’s reading, products with either label could share a shelf for a while. Which date name you see now depends on the food; the table shows the main cases (it is not a complete list).

| On the package | Meaning | What the law and notices say |
|---|---|---|
| 소비기한 | The date through which food is safe to eat if stored as directed | A date item named in the Act. The notice calls for it for most food types (confectionery, bread and rice cakes, noodles, tofu, dairy (other than refrigerated milk, see the next row) and others); a few types use a manufacturing date (ice cream, sugar, salt) or let the maker choose between 소비기한 and a quality-retention date |
| 유통기한 | The period in which sale is permitted | Refrigerated milk (in the notice’s classification, milk and recombined milk). Because the rules let this category wait until products made, processed or loaded for import on or after 1 January 2031 before applying 소비기한, 유통기한 may still appear until then |
| 품질유지기한 (quality-retention date) | The date until which food keeps its own quality under suitable storage; not defined as the date it is safe to eat | Types where the notice lets the maker print 소비기한 or a quality-retention date: soy pastes and sauces (not fermented soybean blocks), vinegar, pickles, jam, beer, starch and flour, salted seafood and others |

The milk exception rests on the Act’s [Addenda, Article 2(2) (Act No. 18445 entry)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsRvsDocListP.do?lsId=013094&chrClsCd=010202&lsRvsGubun=all) and the [enforcement rule’s addenda, Article 2(2) (Ordinance No. 1813 entry)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsRvsDocListP.do?lsId=013475&chrClsCd=010202&lsRvsGubun=all). The Act lets items that need better refrigerated-storage standards start applying the new date item from a date set by Prime Minister’s Ordinance, within eight years of the Act taking effect; the rule names those items as products in the “milk” category (우유류), refrigerated products only, and sets the date at 1 January 2031. The labeling standards notice files milk and recombined milk under one food type, and fortified, lactic-acid-bacteria, lactose-free and processed milk under another. The addenda to the notice on setting 소비기한 (Article 2(2)) point to the same classification, naming the milk in Part III, 1, 머, 1) 가) of the labeling standards notice (pasteurized products only) as the item concerned. The enforcement rule itself says what counts as milk by reference to a different notice, the one on processing standards and component specifications for livestock products (축산물 가공기준 및 성분규격), whose text could not be opened. On the labeling standards notice’s classification, fortified and processed milk do not appear to fall under the exception. That is this guide’s reading, so check the date name printed on the individual package.

So the date number alone is not enough. Read the words printed beside it, such as 소비기한, 유통기한, or a quality-retention date, and check the product’s storage directions. For imported food, go by the date name on the Korean-language label rather than translating the original wording yourself. The notice gives Use by date, Expiration date and EXP as examples of English names for 소비기한, and Best before date and Best before for a quality-retention date, and it says a Korean sticker must not cover the date printed on the original package.

## Storage instructions are part of the date

A 소비기한 assumes that the product has been stored as directed, because the Act defines it that way. A refrigerated item left at room temperature for a long period has not necessarily been kept under the conditions used for its printed date. Check the storage wording when shopping, put chilled or frozen products away as directed, and follow any separate instructions for opened packages; an opened package is no longer in the state the date was set for (this guide’s reading). If an opened food comes with no instructions for after opening, this guide recommends eating it as soon as you reasonably can (this guide’s recommendation). The labeling standards notice also has makers print the storage method where it applies, and requires chilled or frozen livestock products to carry “refrigerated” or “frozen” on the main display panel (unless the storage method already appears on that panel or “refrigerated” or “frozen” is part of the product name).

The temperature bands are in the [notice on setting 소비기한](https://www.law.go.kr/%ED%96%89%EC%A0%95%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99/%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%2C%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%EC%B2%A8%EA%B0%80%EB%AC%BC%2C%EC%B6%95%EC%82%B0%EB%AC%BC%EB%B0%8F%EA%B1%B4%EA%B0%95%EA%B8%B0%EB%8A%A5%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%EC%9D%98%EC%86%8C%EB%B9%84%EA%B8%B0%ED%95%9C%EC%84%A4%EC%A0%95%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80) (소비기한 설정기준). It has makers test shelf life at the temperatures a product meets in distribution and defines 실온 (room temperature) as 1–35°C, 상온 (ordinary temperature) as 15–25°C, refrigerated as 0–10°C and frozen as −18°C or below (Article 7(2); for chilled and frozen foods, conditions set in the food standards take precedence). It has makers include 35°C for 실온 products, 25°C for 상온 products and 10°C for refrigerated products among their test temperatures. Read each of those as the upper end a business must cover when it sets a date, not as a guarantee that the food is fine at home up to that temperature (this guide’s reading). Follow the temperature and method printed on the product.

The date is set by the business, product by product. Under the same notice, manufacturers test for a quality-and-safety limit period, taking account of packaging, storage conditions, method and recipe, and then set the 소비기한 within it under real distribution conditions (Articles 3 and 4(1)). The notice also defines a “recommended” 소비기한 (권장소비기한), a figure businesses may consult, and in some cases, such as staying within it, lets them skip their own tests (Articles 2(3) and 12). Such reference figures help a business date its own product; they are not a chart for stretching the date on a particular brand, package or home storage history (this guide’s reading). Adding a category’s average number of days from an online chart to your own product does not verify that product’s safety.

## A quick check at home

1. Read whether the printed date is a 소비기한, 유통기한, or another type of date. Refrigerated milk may still carry 유통기한, and foods such as soy sauce and vinegar may carry a quality-retention date.
2. Check the product’s refrigeration or room-temperature instructions and any directions for after opening.
3. If the package is damaged or the food was stored differently from the label, do not rely on the date alone.
4. If a date that marks an end point (소비기한, 유통기한 or a quality-retention date) has passed, this guide recommends not eating the food (this guide’s recommendation). The Act defines 소비기한 as the date up to which food is safe when stored as labeled, so after it nothing on the package vouches for the food. The sale-permission date (유통기한) and the quality-retention date are not described as dates that speak for safety afterwards (this guide’s reading), so the same caution applies to refrigerated milk marked with 유통기한 or a soy sauce marked with a quality-retention date. If you are unsure, do not extend the date using category averages or smell. Check the product instructions and current official food-safety guidance.

Date labels do not mean every food can be handled the same way. The package and its storage directions are the practical starting point; a general article cannot assess an individual product.

This guide follows the statutes and notices as read on the National Law Information Center website on 9 November 2026. The Act in force on that day is the version effective 19 September 2025 (Act No. 20826); an amendment due to take effect on 27 November 2026 (Act No. 21707) concerns advertising and has nothing to do with date labeling. The list of amendments to the enforcement rule (up to Prime Minister’s Ordinance No. 2049) contains none that changes the date 1 January 2031. The labeling standards notice is the 12 May 2026 amendment (No. 2026-37, mostly effective 1 January 2028), whose statement of reasons does not touch date labeling, although the cited provisions were not compared with the version now in force; the notice on setting 소비기한 is the 20 April 2022 version (No. 2022-31).
