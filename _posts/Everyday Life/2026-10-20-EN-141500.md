If a date printed on food has passed, a smell test or an online chart saying “a few more days” cannot establish that the product is safe. On Korean labels, **유통기한 (yutong-gihan)** refers to the period in which a product may be sold, while **소비기한 (sobi-gihan)** is the date through which it can be safely consumed when the stated storage instructions are followed. They answer different questions.

## Read the label next to the date

South Korea began implementing its shift from use of the sale-oriented 유통기한 to the consumer-oriented 소비기한 in 2023. The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) describes the former as a period when a business may sell a product and the latter as a safe-consumption period under the stated storage conditions. During the initial transition, the MFDS said both kinds of packaging could appear in stores while companies used existing packaging materials. See the [MFDS explanation of the change](https://www.mfds.go.kr/brd/m_580/view.do?seq=81).

The date number alone is not enough. Read the words printed beside it, such as 소비기한, 유통기한, or a quality-retention date, and check the product’s storage directions. Labels used in the United States, the United Kingdom, and other countries do not map neatly onto Korean terms; this guide describes labels used in South Korea.

## Storage instructions are part of the date

A 소비기한 assumes that the product has been stored as directed. A refrigerated item left at room temperature for a long period has not necessarily been kept under the conditions used for its printed date. Check the storage wording when shopping, put chilled or frozen products away as directed, and follow any separate instructions for opened packages. The MFDS guidance discusses date checks together with food-specific storage temperatures and methods.

Food Safety Korea publishes guidance and reference material on setting use-by dates for food categories. Its [summary of reference values](https://www.foodsafetykorea.go.kr/portal/board/boardDetail.do?bbs_no=BBS190405&menu_grp=MENU_NEW05&menu_no=3852&ntctxt_no=1092541) reports experimental results across selected categories and products. Those averages are not an extension calculator for a particular brand, package, or household storage history. Adding a number of days from a chart to your milk or tofu does not verify that product’s safety.

## A quick check at home

1. Read whether the printed date is a 소비기한, 유통기한, or another type of date.
2. Check the product’s refrigeration or room-temperature instructions and any directions for after opening.
3. If the package is damaged or the food was stored differently from the label, do not rely on the date alone.
4. If you are unsure whether food is safe after its 소비기한, do not extend it using category averages or smell. Check the product instructions and current official food-safety guidance.

Date labels do not mean every food can be handled the same way. The package and its storage directions are the practical starting point; a general article cannot assess an individual product.
